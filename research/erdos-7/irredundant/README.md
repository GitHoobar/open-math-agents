# Irredundant covering systems with arbitrarily negative coprime-triple estimates

**GitHoobar · 27 November 2026 · developed with OpenAI Codex.**

Can a proposed upper bound on the fraction of integers covered by arithmetic progressions fail even when the progressions cover **every** integer and **each** progression is indispensable? Yes. For every real number $R$, we construct a finite family of residue classes with distinct moduli greater than one whose true covered fraction is 1 and whose proposed upper estimate is less than $R$. [Two-page proof](./irredundant-covers.pdf) · [LaTeX source](./irredundant-covers.tex)

## The open question

Harrington, Klein, Lowrance and Trifonov asked whether a coverage estimate they proved under restrictions on the prime divisors of the moduli held in general. It counts the reciprocal moduli, subtracts reciprocal products for coprime pairs, and adds reciprocal products for pairwise-coprime triples. [Theorem 1.9 and Problem 4](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.18644)

Filip Majchrzak already disproved the unrestricted estimate. He gave full covers containing removable classes and irredundant examples that leave some integers uncovered. Page 1 of his November revision says, “The case of irredundant full covers is left open here.” The theorem in this note answers that specific question. [Majchrzak, revision 2, page 1](https://zenodo.org/api/records/24408596/files/Majchrzak_HKLT_Problem4.pdf/content)

## Construction and proof

Start with classes $0\pmod{3q}$ for distinct primes $q>5$. Each has a private integer, $3q$. Their reciprocal moduli can sum to an arbitrarily large number by Euler's theorem on the reciprocals of the primes. We keep all these classes. We adapt Majchrzak's binary construction to cover all but one branch modulo a power of two, then cover four of the five residues modulo five on that branch. For each hole in the original odd period, we add a class that catches the hole on the fifth residue. The two-page proof gives an explicit private integer for every class by specifying its three residues and applying the Chinese remainder theorem.

The only coprime pairs join a class in the binary or mixed block to an original odd class. No three moduli are pairwise coprime. If $a,b,c$ are the sums of reciprocal moduli in the binary and mixed block, original odd block, and repair block, respectively, the proposed estimate is exactly $1-(a-1)(b-1)+c$. Here $a-1\ge1/8$, $c<1/(5N)$ for odd period $N\ge21$, and $b$ is unbounded. For instance, taking primes in increasing order above five until $b>2$ guarantees an estimate below 1. The proof gives examples below any prescribed negative threshold.

## A general extension observation

The private-witness construction also gives a simple extension rule. Suppose a finite irredundant family of classes with distinct odd moduli greater than one has odd common period $N>1$ and misses residues $u_1,\ldots,u_h$ modulo $N$, where $h>0$. It can be extended to an irredundant full cover while retaining all the original classes. For $1\le i\le h$ add the class $2^{i-1}-1\pmod{2^i}$ and the class given by $x\equiv-1\pmod{2^i}$, $x\equiv u_i\pmod N$. The new moduli have odd parts 1 and $N$, respectively, and are all distinct. The binary classes cover all points outside the branch $-1\pmod{2^h}$. On that branch, the original classes and the repairs cover every point. Each old private residue stays private on the branch, and each hole there belongs to just its own repair. Each binary class has a private point over $u_h$ off the branch. The fifth residue used in the main theorem also controls the coprime-pair sum.

## Verification and provenance

The [explicit finite example](./concrete_example.py) also gives a violation without using prime-reciprocal divergence. Its starting moduli are the numbers $3k$ for odd $1\le k\le127$ with $29\nmid k$, omitting 315. The script specifies their residues and checks a private integer for each of these 61 classes. It finds 127 distinct holes, enough to use 29 in place of five and 28 mixed classes in place of four. Specifically, on the surviving binary branch the mixed classes use the residues 0 through 27 modulo 29, and the repairs use residue 28. Every odd modulus is divisible by three and coprime to 29, so the same coverage proof, private-witness table and score formula apply. Exact rational arithmetic gives a score below $1{,}999{,}869/2{,}000{,}000=0.9999345$ for the completed full cover. Run `python3 -B concrete_example.py` to reproduce that certificate. The complete cover includes the new binary, mixed and repair classes as well as the 61 original classes.
